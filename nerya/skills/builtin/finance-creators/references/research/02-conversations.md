# Conversations and Long-Form Interviews

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-07-27**.

Inclusion rule: prioritize conversations, panels, interviews, or live education
of roughly twenty minutes or longer. Short television hits and promotional
clips are excluded as evidence for deep reasoning. Where a full transcript was
not available, only official title, channel, duration, description, and format
are treated as direct evidence; content-level conclusions are explicitly
labelled `framework inference`.

## Unusual Whales (`@unusual_whales`)

Unlike the other two subjects, Unusual Whales has a substantial official
long-form channel. It is a brand with rotating hosts, team members, panels, and
guests; no episode should be treated as the private reasoning of a single
founder.

### Repeated conversation formats

1. **Event-driven macro panel.** Episode 16 runs 1:26:12 and frames a live panel
   around a CPI print and the 2023 macro outlook. [FC-UW-01] The official
   metadata establishes the event, duration, and panel format; without a
   verified transcript, individual macro claims should remain guest-specific.
2. **Policy-to-market scenario discussion.** Episode 58 runs 1:20:54 and brings
   a macro panel to rate-pause, recession, tariff, and 2025-outlook questions.
   [FC-UW-02] Repetition across years supports the limited inference that the
   brand prefers scenario panels around scheduled policy events rather than one
   permanent house forecast.
3. **Live tool interpretation.** A 1:02:36 official education/request stream
   has team members interpret options flow in real time. [FC-UW-03] This is the
   closest conversation evidence for an operational protocol: answer a ticker
   request, inspect the live feed, and explain ambiguity instead of issuing a
   detached prediction.
4. **Sponsored thematic panel.** Episode 31 runs 1:32:23 and names “Sponsored
   by Verses AI” in its official title while discussing AI, finance, and the
   future of the sector. [FC-UW-04] Sponsorship is disclosed, but it creates a
   material topic-selection and guest-framing conflict.

### Conversation-level mental models

- **Framework inference — live evidence before narrative:** event panels and
  flow-request streams are organized around data releases or observable tape,
  then invite interpretation.
- **Framework inference — disagreement is a feature:** panels can expose
  competing macro paths. A downstream Skill should preserve named guest views
  rather than blend them into “Unusual Whales believes.”
- **Framework inference — education and product use are intertwined:** live
  analysis teaches concepts while demonstrating a subscription product. This
  can make the workflow concrete, but also gives the brand a commercial reason
  to emphasize actionable-looking signals.

### Contradictions and attribution controls

- A data-platform identity encourages probabilistic, tool-led reasoning, while
  episode titles often use decisive attention hooks. Do not copy the headline
  certainty into the analytical output.
- The sponsored AI episode is primary evidence of a disclosed commercial
  relationship; it is not evidence that any conclusion was purchased.
- Hosts, staff educators, and invited experts have different epistemic status.
  Attribute a claim to the actual speaker when a transcript is available;
  otherwise cite only the episode's topic and format.
- Live panels can preserve spontaneous reasoning but also include performance,
  audience, and production incentives. They are not audited trading records.

**Dimension strength: medium for brand format; weak for a singular founder
persona.** The strongest defensible traits are panel-based scenario analysis,
event anchoring, and live data-tool education.

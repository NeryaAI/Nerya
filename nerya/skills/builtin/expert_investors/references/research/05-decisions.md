# Decision Records and Retrospectives

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-07-27**. This file emphasizes observable decisions and first-party hindsight. Facts, the subject's retrospective, and researcher inference are separated.

## Warren Buffett

### Dexter Shoe acquisition (1993; retrospective in 2007)

- **Fact:** Berkshire acquired Dexter Shoe using Berkshire stock rather than cash.
- **First-party retrospective:** Buffett wrote that Dexter's value went to zero and that issuing Berkshire shares made the mistake vastly more expensive; he classified it among his worst decisions.
- **Inference:** Buffett failed twice: he overestimated the durability of a domestic footwear moat, then funded the error with an appreciating asset. The second error dominated the first.
- **Reusable check:** When paying with equity, value the consideration at plausible future opportunity cost, not only at today's quoted value.
- Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf — **official shareholder letter; primary/highest credibility; accessed 2026-07-27**.

### Precision Castparts acquisition (2016; retrospective in 2020)

- **Fact:** Berkshire purchased Precision Castparts and later recorded a large impairment.
- **First-party retrospective:** Buffett said he had been right about the quality of the business and wrong about its normalized earnings, and therefore paid too much.
- **Inference:** “Excellent business” and “excellent investment” separated at the purchase price. Operational admiration likely anchored the valuation.
- **Reusable check:** Write a normalized-earnings range before discussing strategic quality; do not let qualitative admiration set the base case.
- Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf — **official shareholder letter; primary/highest credibility; accessed 2026-07-27**.

## Aswath Damodaran

### Tesla valuation and purchase discipline (2017)

- **Fact:** Damodaran published a narrative-driven Tesla valuation, disclosed a buy threshold, and described buying after the market price crossed into his valuation range.
- **First-party rationale:** The purchase followed a revised operating story and explicit value estimate, not a claim that Tesla was conventionally cheap.
- **Inference:** This is a clean example of translating story into drivers and drivers into a conditional action. It is not proof that the chosen story was correct.
- **Reusable check:** State the story, the variables that must become true, the value distribution, and the price that provides sufficient error room.
- Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/08/tesla-story-and-valuation-redux.html — **author's contemporaneous blog; primary/high credibility for his own process; accessed 2026-07-27**.

### Nvidia sale and retrospective opportunity cost (2017-2023)

- **Fact:** Damodaran described Nvidia as a successful investment but acknowledged selling as price outran his valuation, before later AI-driven appreciation.
- **First-party retrospective:** He did not reinterpret the missed upside as proof that valuation was useless; he treated it as evidence that story and market possibility can expand beyond a prior model.
- **Inference:** A sell discipline protects against paying any price, but a single-point valuation can truncate exposure to positive convexity. Position sizing or staged exits may express uncertainty better than an all-or-nothing answer.
- Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/06/nvidia-storytelling-and-valuation.html — **author's retrospective; primary/high credibility for his own account; accessed 2026-07-27**.

## Howard Marks

### Buying into the 2008 panic

- **Fact:** In “The Limits to Negativism,” Marks argued during the crisis that indiscriminate pessimism and forced selling had created unusually attractive prospective returns in distressed credit.
- **First-party reasoning:** He did not claim certainty about the bottom. The case was that prices already reflected disaster and the asymmetry had improved enough to act.
- **Inference:** The decision was a price-and-positioning judgment under uncertainty, not an economic forecast. Liquidity and client capital made it executable.
- **Reusable check:** Ask what outcome the price discounts, who is forced to sell, and whether survival time exceeds the market's liquidation horizon.
- Source: https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-limits-to-negativism — **contemporaneous official memo; primary/highest credibility for stated reasoning; accessed 2026-07-27**.

### Declaring a credit-market “Sea Change” (2022)

- **Fact:** Marks argued that the long regime of falling rates and easy money had changed, raising the prospective return available from credit and altering asset-allocation tradeoffs.
- **First-party reasoning:** The memo was a regime update rather than a precise rate forecast.
- **Inference:** His method permits a large strategic shift without pretending to know the next macro print. The risk is treating a regime label as permanent after the evidence changes.
- **Reusable check:** Separate slow structural variables from fast cyclical variables and specify what would falsify the regime thesis.
- Source: https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change — **official memo; primary/highest credibility for stated thesis; accessed 2026-07-27**.

## Michael Mauboussin

### Building an expectations-and-base-rates research program

- **Fact:** Across Legg Mason, Credit Suisse, and Morgan Stanley, Mauboussin repeatedly organized investment research around market-implied expectations, competitive-advantage period, capital allocation, and reference-class data.
- **Observable decision:** This is a career and research-allocation decision, not a disclosed security trade. The public record supports the choice to build reusable decision infrastructure; it does not support attributing a portfolio return to him.
- **Inference:** The persistence of the program across institutions suggests a durable belief: improve the process by making expectations and base rates explicit.
- **Reusable check:** Reverse-engineer what the current price requires, select a relevant reference class, then identify which company-specific evidence justifies departing from the base rate.

## Stanley Druckenmiller

### Sterling short (1992)

- **Fact:** While managing money for George Soros, Druckenmiller developed the thesis that sterling could not remain inside the European Exchange Rate Mechanism at the prevailing policy settings; the position was enlarged before Britain's exit.
- **First-party retrospective:** His later accounts emphasize Soros's push to size the position far larger once the asymmetry was clear.
- **Inference:** Druckenmiller supplied the thesis; Soros sharpened the sizing lesson. The edge combined policy inconsistency, a catalyst, liquidity, and concentration.
- **Reusable check:** A large position requires not only high confidence, but also a visible policy constraint/catalyst and tolerable loss if timing is wrong.
- Supporting first-party interview source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-7sWLIybWnQ — **long-form interview by the Sohn Conference Foundation; primary for Druckenmiller's decision philosophy, medium for historical detail not fully reproduced there; accessed 2026-07-27**.

### Dot-com reversal and loss (2000)

- **Fact:** Druckenmiller has repeatedly recounted being bearish on technology valuations, watching less experienced managers profit, abandoning his own view near the peak, and then losing billions when the bubble broke.
- **First-party retrospective:** He frames the episode as emotional and undisciplined rather than as an unforeseeable macro shock.
- **Inference:** The failure was process override: price action and comparison anxiety converted a defensible thesis into a late, concentrated chase.
- **Reusable check:** Before increasing concentration, record whether the thesis improved or whether only the price moved. If only price moved, force a cooling-off period and independent review.
- Context and current disclosure boundary: https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1536411&owner=exclude — **regulatory primary source for later reportable long holdings only; not evidence of the 2000 trade itself; accessed 2026-07-27**.

# Compact Timelines

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-07-27**. Timelines include only milestones relevant to the experts' investment or valuation operating systems. Dates from employer biographies establish roles; first-party letters, memos, and research establish ideas and retrospective judgments.

## Warren Buffett

| Period | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1956-1969 | Buffett Partnership era | Concentrated Graham-influenced investing and the early capital-allocation record. |
| 1965 onward | Control of Berkshire Hathaway | Permanent capital becomes the institutional advantage; the textile acquisition later becomes a canonical mistake. |
| 1993 | Dexter Shoe acquired with Berkshire shares | Later judged a double error: moat failure plus exceptionally costly equity consideration. |
| 2016 | Precision Castparts acquired | Later impairment demonstrates that business quality cannot rescue an excessive price. |
| 2020-2021 | Buffett publicly owns the Precision Castparts error | Candid retrospective becomes part of the method, not merely reputation management. |

- 2020 letter: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf — **official primary source; accessed 2026-07-27**.

## Aswath Damodaran

| Period | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1980s onward | Joins NYU Stern and develops a public valuation curriculum | Establishes valuation as a teachable, inspectable process rather than a proprietary black box. |
| 1990s-2010s | Publishes valuation books, datasets, spreadsheets, and classes | Creates a continually updated public infrastructure for corporate finance and valuation. |
| 2017 | Publishes revised Tesla story/valuation and a price-contingent investment decision | Canonical demonstration of “story to numbers to action.” |
| 2017-2023 | Owns, revalues, and ultimately exits Nvidia before its full AI rerating | Canonical demonstration of disciplined selling and the opportunity cost of model-bounded value. |
| 2020s | Continues updating country risk premiums and market datasets | Keeps the process empirical and date-stamped rather than timeless. |

Sources:

- Tesla retrospective: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2017/08/tesla-story-and-valuation-redux.html — **first-party; accessed 2026-07-27**.
- Nvidia retrospective: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2023/06/nvidia-storytelling-and-valuation.html — **first-party; accessed 2026-07-27**.

## Howard Marks

| Period | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1978 | Moves into high-yield bonds at Citicorp | Begins a career in securities then considered below conventional institutional quality. |
| 1980s | Builds distressed-debt capability at TCW | Converts risk aversion into price-sensitive credit specialization. |
| 1995 | Co-founds Oaktree Capital Management | Institutionalizes risk control, specialization, and cycle awareness. |
| October 2008 | Publishes “The Limits to Negativism” during the crisis | Contemporaneous evidence of contrarian action without a claimed bottom forecast. |
| 2022 | Publishes “Sea Change” | Updates the framework for a prospective higher-rate, higher-credit-return regime. |

## Michael Mauboussin

| Period | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1980s-2000s | Research and strategy work at Drexel Burnham Lambert and Legg Mason | Develops the expectations-investing and multidisciplinary research program. |
| 1993 onward | Teaches at Columbia Business School | Makes process, probability, and competitive-advantage reasoning central to investor education. |
| 2000s | Publishes *Expectations Investing*, *More Than You Know*, and related work | Codifies reverse DCF, complex adaptive systems, and decision-process thinking. |
| 2013 | Joins Credit Suisse | Continues the research-led role at a global investment bank. |
| 2020 onward | Leads Consilient Research at Morgan Stanley Investment Management | Extends the program into base rates, capital allocation, and competitive-advantage research. |

## Stanley Druckenmiller

| Period | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1981 | Founds Duquesne Capital Management | Creates the vehicle for a concentrated, cross-asset macro process. |
| 1988-2000 | Manages money for George Soros / Quantum Fund while running Duquesne | Combines independent thesis generation with Soros's aggressive sizing discipline. |
| 1992 | Builds the sterling short before Britain leaves the ERM | Canonical policy-inconsistency trade with a catalyst and concentrated sizing. |
| 2000 | Chases technology near the peak and suffers a multibillion-dollar reversal | Canonical first-party example of emotion overriding a correct prior view. |
| 2010 | Returns outside capital and converts Duquesne to a family office | Ends the public fund chapter while retaining flexibility to trade across regimes. |
| 2022 | Public Sohn conversation on inflation, liquidity, and regime risk | Shows the continuing emphasis on central-bank liquidity and forward conditions. |

Sources:

- Sohn Conference Foundation interview: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-7sWLIybWnQ — **first-party long-form interview; high credibility for current reasoning, accessed 2026-07-27**.
- SEC Duquesne Family Office filing index: https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1536411&owner=exclude — **regulatory primary source for the modern entity and disclosed U.S. long holdings; accessed 2026-07-27**.

## Boundary note

These timelines are not performance league tables. Buffett's corporate letters, Damodaran's public valuations, and Marks's dated memos offer unusually good decision evidence. Mauboussin's public evidence is primarily research and career allocation, not an audited personal portfolio. Druckenmiller's public filings cover only a subset of exposures; historical macro trades rely materially on his later recollections. Those differences must remain visible when the five perspectives are combined.

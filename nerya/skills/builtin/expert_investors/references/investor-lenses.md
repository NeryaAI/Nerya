# Source-Backed Investor Lenses

Use these as distinct analytical modes. Do not impersonate the named person,
invent a quotation, or imply endorsement. A statement supported by the source
register may use its source ID. Applying that model to a new asset must be
labelled `framework inference`.

## Lens Router

| Decision | Primary lens | Useful challenger |
|---|---|---|
| Durable business quality and capital allocation | Buffett | Mauboussin |
| Intrinsic value or a disputed DCF | Damodaran | Marks |
| Market temperature, credit, or permanent-loss risk | Marks | Druckenmiller |
| Price-implied expectations or forecast quality | Mauboussin | Damodaran |
| Tactical macro, liquidity, or position expression | Druckenmiller | Marks |

Run one lens by default and no more than three unless the user explicitly asks
for a full committee. State why each lens was selected.

## Warren Buffett: Business Owner Lens

**Distinctive question:** What durable owner cash can this understandable
business produce, and is the price rational relative to alternatives?

### Core models

1. **Owner earnings.** Reconstruct cash available to owners after maintenance
   capital and working-capital needs; accounting earnings are only a starting
   point. [B1]
2. **Quality before cheapness.** Favor durable economics, pricing power, and a
   long reinvestment runway over a low multiple attached to a structurally poor
   business. Price still determines the return. [B2, B6]
3. **Intrinsic value is an estimate.** Value is discounted future owner cash,
   not book value or a precise observable fact. Use a range and a margin for
   estimation error. [B3]
4. **Circle of competence.** Define what can and cannot be understood. A small,
   honest boundary is better than broad but shallow coverage. [B4]

### Decision heuristics

- Analyze the business as if buying the whole company for a long holding
  period.
- Compare every use of capital with its best alternative, including the future
  cost of issuing shares. Dexter Shoe is the canonical safeguard. [B5]
- Separate business admiration from purchase discipline. Precision Castparts
  shows that a good company can still be an overpaid investment. [B6]
- Concentrate only when durability, comprehension, value, and survivability all
  align. Confidence alone is not evidence.

### Reasoning voice

Use plain business language, one concrete example, and a compact rule. State
what is outside the competence boundary. Admit a judgment error directly; do
not convert hindsight into inevitability. [B5, B6, B7]

### Failure boundaries

- Weak for assets without owner cash flows and early businesses whose economics
  cannot yet be bounded.
- Quality can be overpaid for; historical moat evidence can miss disruption.
- Do not turn patience into a rule that forbids thesis-driven selling.

## Aswath Damodaran: Narrative Valuer Lens

**Distinctive question:** What business story is encoded in the valuation, and
can every important part of that story survive numerical translation?

### Core models

1. **Story-to-number bridge.** Map narrative claims to market size, revenue,
   margins, reinvestment, risk, and competitive fade. A story without numerical
   consequences is advocacy. [D1, D4]
2. **Growth needs excess returns.** Growth creates value only when incremental
   return on invested capital exceeds the relevant cost of capital after the
   reinvestment required to produce that growth. [D1, D2]
3. **Pricing is not valuation.** A multiple may explain what others pay; it does
   not by itself estimate cash-flow value. Name which task is being performed.
   [D2, D7]
4. **Transparent uncertainty.** Expose assumptions, scenarios, and sensitivity.
   The spreadsheet documents a conditional claim; it does not remove bias or
   uncertainty. [D1, D4]

### Decision heuristics

- Write the narrative before the forecast and reject mutually inconsistent
  assumptions.
- Show the three inputs that dominate value and a range around each.
- Update the story and the model together when evidence changes.
- Prefer staged entry or exit when uncertainty is wide. The Tesla and Nvidia
  retrospectives show both discipline and the cost of model-bounded upside.
  [D5, D6]

### Reasoning voice

Use an energetic teacher's structure: define the category, expose the causal
chain, translate it into numbers, then challenge the most fragile assumption.
Avoid decorative decimals and professional mystique. [D7, D8]

### Failure boundaries

- Terminal value and distant cash flows can dominate the result.
- Intangible accounting can distort invested capital and historical ROIC.
- A transparent model is auditable, not uniquely true.

## Howard Marks: Cycle and Risk Lens

**Distinctive question:** What can go wrong, how much optimism or fear is
already in the price, and are we being paid for the full outcome distribution?

### Core models

1. **Second-level comparison.** Asset quality is insufficient; compare price,
   consensus expectations, and the likely range of outcomes.
2. **Risk precedes the outcome.** Risk is the possibility and severity of loss,
   influenced by price, leverage, fundamentals, and future states. It is not
   synonymous with observed volatility. [H2]
3. **Cycles and pendulums.** Psychology, credit availability, and risk tolerance
   overshoot. Calibrate aggressiveness without pretending to know the exact
   turning point. [H4, H6]
4. **Epistemic humility.** Replace a point forecast with scenarios and portfolio
   resilience. Consequential regime judgments remain probabilistic tilts, not
   clairvoyance. [H1, H3, H4]

### Decision heuristics

- Separate possible, probable, and knowable.
- Ask who is forced to transact and what outcome the current price discounts.
- Move between offense and defense by degrees, not with an all-or-nothing call.
- State the cost of being early and late. The 2008 memo is evidence for acting
  on asymmetry without claiming the bottom. [H5]

### Reasoning voice

Define the contested term, present the conventional view, turn it over, and end
with a calibrated conclusion. Use paired contrasts only when they clarify a
real trade-off. Include the strongest opposing case. [H3, H6]

### Failure boundaries

- Cycle awareness does not produce precise timing.
- Qualitative caution can be hard to falsify and can lag innovation.
- A regime label must include evidence that would retire it.

## Michael Mauboussin: Expectations and Base-Rates Lens

**Distinctive question:** What performance is embedded in price, what happened
in the relevant reference class, and is the decision process sound apart from
the realized outcome?

### Core models

1. **Expectations investing.** Reverse-engineer the sales, margins,
   reinvestment, and competitive-advantage period implied by price, then test
   which expectations can plausibly change. [M1, M2]
2. **Outside view plus inside view.** Start with a defensible reference class,
   inspect its distribution, then require case-specific evidence for departing
   from the base rate. [M4]
3. **Process is not outcome.** In a luck-heavy domain, one result is weak
   evidence. Record probabilities, alternatives, sizing, and disconfirming
   evidence before the outcome. [M1, M5]
4. **Moat as value-creation duration.** Analyze industry structure, ROIC,
   reinvestment, and competitive fade instead of assigning a qualitative moat
   label. [M2, M3]

### Decision heuristics

- Choose and defend the reference class before quoting its base rate.
- Keep market-implied expectations and the analyst's forecast side by side.
- Diagnose sample size, incentives, feedback, and causal mechanism before
  judging a track record.
- Use cross-disciplinary analogies only when they preserve the causal structure
  of the investment problem.

### Reasoning voice

Classify the domain, define the mechanism, introduce the relevant distribution,
and return to a probabilistic investment test. Mark disagreement between the
outside and inside views instead of averaging them blindly. [M5]

### Failure boundaries

- Reference classes can be selected opportunistically.
- Reverse valuation exposes required assumptions but does not identify which
  one is wrong.
- Public evidence supports a research persona, not an audited personal trading
  record. Never invent representative Mauboussin trades.

## Stanley Druckenmiller: Adaptive Macro Lens

**Distinctive question:** What future inflection in liquidity, policy, or
earnings is the market discounting, how should it be expressed, and what signal
forces a reversal?

### Core models

1. **Markets discount the future.** Build 12-24 month paths for earnings,
   policy, and liquidity rather than extrapolating the current snapshot. [S1,
   S3]
2. **Policy and liquidity transmit across assets.** Specify the measure,
   reaction function, transmission channel, and horizon; do not use liquidity
   as a catch-all explanation. [S1, S2, S3]
3. **Conviction affects size.** Rare opportunities with strong asymmetry may
   justify concentration, but only with explicit loss capacity, liquidity, and
   invalidation. [S1, S3]
4. **Flexibility is risk management.** A strong view is provisional. Change the
   position when facts or market behavior invalidate it instead of defending
   personal consistency. [S1, S4]

### Decision heuristics

- Translate the macro thesis into a specific instrument and catalyst.
- Distinguish an improved thesis from a price move that merely creates urgency.
- For a large position, document maximum loss, liquidity, hidden correlation,
  and the exit signal before entry.
- Treat the 2000 technology reversal as the canonical warning: concentration
  magnifies emotional contamination as efficiently as insight. [S1]

### Reasoning voice

State the variant view, evidence, expression vehicle, position implication, and
blunt invalidation condition. Use strong but explicitly provisional language.
Admit a failed view and change the position without narrative repair. [S1, S4]

### Failure boundaries

- Much of the edge is tacit timing and pattern recognition that a playbook
  cannot reproduce.
- Form 13F shows only reportable U.S. long positions, not the complete macro
  book, shorts, cash, derivatives, or intra-quarter changes. [S6]
- Do not imitate leverage or concentration without institutional risk controls.

## Disagreement Matrix

Preserve these tensions rather than blending them into generic advice:

| Tension | Route by |
|---|---|
| Buffett long-duration ownership vs. Druckenmiller active reversal | Horizon, instrument, and evidence cadence |
| Damodaran explicit forecasts vs. Marks forecast humility | Scenario width and cost of estimation error |
| Mauboussin base rates vs. company-specific narrative | Reference-class fit and structural change evidence |
| Marks measured posture vs. Druckenmiller concentration | Conviction, catalyst, liquidity, and loss capacity |

When lenses disagree, return the disagreement, the assumption causing it, and
the evidence that would resolve it. Do not vote by majority.

## Citation Rules

- Cite at least one source ID for each named lens used.
- Use quotation marks only after verifying exact words in the cited primary
  source. Otherwise paraphrase and label the conclusion `framework inference`.
- Attach an `as_of` date to current market facts; the sources below support the
  framework, not today's price or fundamentals.
- For deeper provenance, load the matching file under `research/`.

# Howard Marks: Cycle and Risk Lens

Shared rules from the `expert_investors` hub apply: no impersonation, no
invented quotations, applications to new assets are `framework inference`,
and a recommendation is never an order.

**Distinctive question:** What can go wrong, how much optimism or fear is
already in the price, and are we being paid for the full outcome distribution?

## Core models

1. **Second-level comparison.** Asset quality is insufficient; compare price,
   consensus expectations, and the likely range of outcomes.
2. **Risk precedes the outcome.** Risk is the possibility and severity of loss,
   influenced by price, leverage, fundamentals, and future states. It is not
   synonymous with observed volatility. [H2]
3. **Cycles and pendulums.** Psychology, credit availability, and risk tolerance
   overshoot. Calibrate aggressiveness without pretending to know the exact
   turning point. [H4, H6]
4. **Epistemic humility.** Replace a point forecast with scenarios and portfolio
   resilience. Consequential regime judgments remain probabilistic tilts, not
   clairvoyance. [H1, H3, H4]

## Decision heuristics

- Separate possible, probable, and knowable.
- Ask who is forced to transact and what outcome the current price discounts.
- Move between offense and defense by degrees, not with an all-or-nothing call.
- State the cost of being early and late. The 2008 memo is evidence for acting
  on asymmetry without claiming the bottom. [H5]

## Reasoning voice

Define the contested term, present the conventional view, turn it over, and end
with a calibrated conclusion. Use paired contrasts only when they clarify a
real trade-off. Include the strongest opposing case. [H3, H6]

## Failure boundaries

- Cycle awareness does not produce precise timing.
- Qualitative caution can be hard to falsify and can lag innovation.
- A regime label must include evidence that would retire it.
